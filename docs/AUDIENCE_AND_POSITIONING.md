# Audience & Positioning — Appreneur Challenge

_Owner-confirmed context. Every copy, design, and marketing decision
must be consistent with this document. Updated together with
`.agents/product-marketing-context.md`._

## Primary audience

- **Business owners age 50+** who want to use AI in their business but
  feel intimidated by technology and don't want to depend on developers.
- **Beginner entrepreneurs** who want to build websites, simple apps,
  or useful digital tools they can offer or sell.

They do **not** consider themselves technical. Many are actively
worried about being "left behind" by AI. Most have never built a
website or app before. Common vocabulary — no-code, wireframe,
user flow, MVP, prompt, deploy — is unfamiliar and can feel
intimidating.

## Two real use cases (never split into two products)

A. **Existing business owner** — build custom websites and useful
internal tools for their own company, scale with AI, and stop paying
developers for every idea.

B. **Entrepreneur** — build websites, simple apps, or digital tools
they can offer or sell as a product/service.

Both audiences are treated as beginners. **Never imply guaranteed
profit, savings, clients, or business results.**

## Pains

- "Technology has always felt intimidating."
- "Hiring a developer is too expensive."
- "I don't know what to build."
- "I don't know where to start."
- "I'm worried I'll get left behind by AI."
- "I've never built anything and I'm too old to start."

## Desired outcomes

- Feel capable — not "become a tech person."
- Learn how to tell AI what you want, in everyday English.
- Finish a real, working first website or app in five days.
- Have a link they can share with a friend, a customer, or their team.
- Know what to do next.

## Objections we must answer

See `src/components/landing/FAQSection.tsx` — the required objection
IDs are the source of truth. At minimum: "am I too old / too
nontechnical", "what if I've never built anything", "do I have to
write code", "can I build for my business", "can I build to sell",
"what if I get stuck".

## Vocabulary

**Use (plain English):**
- "Build by describing what you want in everyday English."
- "Pages and buttons that work."
- "Simple sketch of your pages."
- "The small first version worth building."
- "Instructions you give the AI."
- "Put it online / share the link."
- "What to improve next."

**Avoid in customer copy** (unless immediately translated):
- no-code, low-code, stack, framework
- wireframe → simple sketch
- user flow → what someone clicks first, next, last
- feature boundary / MVP → the small first version
- prompt → instructions you give the AI
- deploy → put it online / share it
- iteration → what to improve next

Internal dev/ops docs may use technical terms freely. Customer-facing
components, emails, and marketing copy may not.

## Brand tone

Empowering, patient, honest, direct-response. Never shame the reader
for being new or older. Never fear-monger about being "left behind."
Frame urgency around learning the new toolset, not scarcity.
